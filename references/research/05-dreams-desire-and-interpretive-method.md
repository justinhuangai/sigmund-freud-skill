# Dreams, desire, and interpretation

## Evidence status

The stored Dreams excerpt contains introductory remarks and prefaces only. It documents Freud's own account of the work and its limitations, not the main chapters on dream-work.

## Dream material

Freud's introductory remarks discuss the limitations of using others' dreams and the privacy costs of using his own. This supports careful attention to context and the limits of the selected material.

## Omission and substitution

The preface acknowledges disguise through omissions and substitutions. An analyst should not pretend such case material is a transparent, unmediated record.

## Wish and interpretation

Do not derive a definite wish from an isolated image. The skill can compare the user's associations with ordinary contextual explanations, without treating symbolism as proof.

## Slips and everyday errors

Everyday Life contains examples of associative interpretation and forgetting. Use them to explain Freud's reasoning historically, while considering fatigue, attention, and chance in contemporary situations.
