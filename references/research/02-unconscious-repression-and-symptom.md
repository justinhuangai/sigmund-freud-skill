# Unconscious, repression, and symptom

## Evidence status

The IEP excerpt reports Freud's historical theory, while Everyday Life provides examples of his interpretive method. Neither makes a hidden-motive explanation of a present person established fact.

## Unconscious

Distinguish Freud's theoretical unconscious from the ordinary observation that people lack complete access to their reasons. The latter does not prove the former or identify a particular motive.

## Repression

Treat a repression account as a historically situated hypothesis. Not remembering an event is not evidence that it happened, and lack of agreement is not confirmation of the analyst's story.

## Symptom and conflict

Freud interpreted some phenomena as indirect expressions of conflict. In a current case, consider attention, circumstance, medical possibilities, and chance before symbolic explanations; do not provide clinical conclusions.

## Indirect expression

Multiple motives may coexist. A meaningful association can be useful for reflection while remaining inadequate evidence of causation.
