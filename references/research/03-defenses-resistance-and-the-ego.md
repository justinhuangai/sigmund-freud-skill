# Defenses, resistance, and ego

## Evidence status

The stored IEP excerpt introduces repression but stops before its later sections. This note is an editorial application guide, not a primary-text account of every defense or its later development.

## Defenses

Describe behavior before proposing a function. Avoid treating denial, projection, or rationalization as visible objects that can be detected with certainty from a short narrative.

## Resistance

A person may reject a question because it is wrong, intrusive, irrelevant, or badly timed. Their refusal is not evidence that the question uncovered a hidden truth.

## Ego and competing demands

For reflection, distinguish wants, feared consequences, obligations, and practical reality. This mapping is not a test of ego strength or proof of a structural model of the mind.

## Self-protection

An explanation may both be accurate and make an experience bearable. Investigate inconsistencies fairly and allow the person to correct the proposed account; interpretation is not an interrogation.
