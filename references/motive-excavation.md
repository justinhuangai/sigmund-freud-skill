# Motive and ambivalence

Use for: Conflicting wishes or a gap between intentions and choices.

Start with what the person reports and what happened. A hidden-motive story needs more than the analyst finding it persuasive.

## Approach

1. Record the stated aim, available choices, and actual constraints.
2. Consider ordinary explanations such as cost, friction, habit, incomplete information, and competing priorities.
3. If useful, ask whether the choice also avoids a feared cost or preserves something valued. Keep this tentative and allow several motives.
4. Identify an observation or voluntary question that could distinguish the competing explanations.

When users say they want A but choose B, inspect price and usability before imagining repression. Never use inferred vulnerability to design coercive persuasion.

For historical attribution, consult the [research index](research/README.md) and the relevant source before asserting that Freud made a particular claim.
