# Transference and repetition

Use for: A recurring interpersonal sequence or reactions linked to earlier relationships.

Do not assume that repetition is caused by childhood or an unconscious wish. Present relationships, power, and actual harmful behavior matter.

## Approach

1. Describe a sequence: situation, expectation, action, response, and outcome.
2. Compare several instances and include exceptions; similar outcomes can have different causes.
3. Ask whether an earlier experience shapes an expectation, without inventing that experience or treating it as the sole cause.
4. If the user wants change, identify a controllable communication or boundary choice and its constraints.

Transference has a clinical history; ordinary relationship analogy is not equivalent to a clinical assessment. Never frame someone's mistreatment as something the harmed person unconsciously chose or deserved.

For historical attribution, consult the [research index](research/README.md) and the relevant source before asserting that Freud made a particular claim.
